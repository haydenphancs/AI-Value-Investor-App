### The non-SUPPORTED items: source passages and fixes

**#4 "opposite" (subtitle, cardSubtitle): IMPRECISE, minor.**
- The article's own caveat says both companies own some bottling and both sell concentrate.
- KO Note 3 p.76: finished product operations were $19,479M, i.e. 41% of KO revenue. PEP Note 1: company-owned bottlers' beverage revenue is 36% of PEP revenue.
- The contrast is real in degree: about 86% of PEP's beverage revenue comes from its own bottlers, and KO's "Most … sold by independent bottling partners". It is not an opposite.
- Fix: subtitle "…two companies that made very different choices about what to own…"; cardSubtitle "Near-identical colas, very different business models behind them."

**#7 KH3: IMPRECISE.** Both filings qualify the claim with "Generally", and the two name different margins.
- KO Note 20 / Item 1: "Generally, finished product operations generate higher net operating revenues but lower gross profit margins than concentrate operations."
- PEP Note 1 fn (a): "Generally, our finished goods beverage operations produce higher net revenue, but lower operating margins as compared to concentrate sold to authorized bottling partners…"
- Fix: "Both filings say finished drinks generally bring more revenue but thinner margins than selling concentrate."

**#14 bottler's agreements: IMPRECISE, minor.** "Each" turns a qualified term into an absolute one.
- KO Item 1 p.5: "Subject to specified terms and conditions and certain variations, the bottler's agreements generally authorize the bottlers to prepare, package, distribute and sell … in (but, subject to applicable local law, generally only in) an identified territory. The bottler is obligated to purchase its entire requirement of concentrates or syrups … from the Company or Company-authorized suppliers."
- Also: "In certain parts of the world outside the United States, we have not granted comprehensive beverage production and distribution rights to the bottlers."
- Fix: "Under bottler's agreements, a bottler generally buys its concentrate from Coca-Cola, adds water and sweetener, packages the drink and sells it in an assigned territory."

**#19 "finished drinks bring in more revenue, but at lower gross margins": IMPRECISE.** Two problems:
- The sentence drops "Generally" (passage quoted under #7).
- It sits directly after "Concentrate made up 59% of … revenue". A listener hears that concentrate brings most of the revenue and then that finished drinks bring in more, which sounds like a contradiction. The filing's comparison is volume for volume. Its own tables show finished product operations at 15% of volume but 41% of revenue (Item 7 p.37).
- Fix: "The filing states the trade-off plainly: finished drinks generally bring in more revenue for the volume sold, but at lower gross margins. They were 15% of its volume but 41% of its revenue."

**#24 "agreed to sell part of its African bottling to one of its largest bottlers": IMPRECISE.**
- KO Note 2 p.74: "In October 2025, the Company entered into a definitive agreement to sell a portion of our interest in our bottling operations in Africa to CCHBC, an equity method investee. Closing is subject to various regulatory approvals and is expected by the end of 2026, upon which we will deconsolidate these bottling operations. We have also agreed to a separate option arrangement for CCHBC to acquire the Company's remaining 25% ownership interest…"
- What is sold is part of its STAKE in the whole African bottling business, and the sale ends Coca-Cola's control. "Part of its African bottling" reads as some of the territories. CCHBC is one of the five largest bottlers listed on p.5, so "one of its largest bottlers" is fine.
- Fix: "…and agreed to sell a controlling stake in its African bottling business to one of its largest bottlers, keeping 25%."

**#46 "and one route into stores for drinks and foods": WRONG (as worded).** The filing describes DSD *systems* in the plural, run by PepsiCo, its independent bottlers and its distributors. In North America, its two biggest units are separate food and beverage businesses, and the filing says integrating their go-to-market is only under evaluation:
- PEP Item 7 Executive Overview p.34: "In North America, we are carefully evaluating an integrated model for our food and beverage supply chains, go-to-market, and commercial capabilities and intend to take a nuanced approach…"
- PEP Item 1 p.4: "We, our independent bottlers and our distributors operate DSD systems that deliver beverages and convenient foods directly to retail stores…"
- Nothing in the filing says drinks and foods share one route. Fix: cut the phrase, or replace it with "…more control over how products reach the shelf, and delivery systems that carry both its drinks and its foods."

   - The cited DSD passage speaks of DSD *systems* run by PepsiCo, its independent bottlers and its distributors. It does not support a single route.
   - The ledger missed the contradicting PEP Item 7 p.34 passage: "In North America, we are carefully evaluating an integrated model for our food and beverage supply chains, go-to-market, and commercial capabilities…"
   - My verdict: WRONG (#46).
2. **Claim 19, African bottling: quoted selectively.**
   - The ledger quotes only the first sentence of Note 2 p.74. It omits "upon which we will deconsolidate these bottling operations" and the option on "the Company's remaining 25% ownership interest".
   - Those omitted lines show the deal hands over control of the whole African business, which "part of its African bottling" misdescribes (#24).
3. **H7 and claim 14, finished vs concentrate margins: the article drops a qualifier the ledger itself quotes.**
   - Both quoted passages open with "Generally", and the article sentences drop it.
   - The ledger also does not address the misreading created by putting "finished drinks bring in more revenue" right after the 59% concentrate share (#7, #19).
4. **Claim 10, bottler's agreements: the same problem.** The ledger quotes "generally authorize" and "from the Company or Company-authorized suppliers", while the article says "each buys its concentrate from Coca-Cola" (#14).
5. **Claim 43, "reliant on bottlers": weakly cited.**
   - The second quote is about *refranchising* partners.
   - The direct support is KO Item 1A p.18: "We rely on our bottling partners for a significant portion of our business. If we are unable to maintain good relationships with our bottling partners, our business could suffer."
   - The claim stands; swap in this quote.
