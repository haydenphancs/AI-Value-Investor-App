## live-core — 7 finding(s)

### R1. [CONFIRMED] [high] Landed records older than the previous trading day get re-sent every 60 s tick (prune only runs after a due fetch; landed_snapshots does not filter by day)

- **Where:** `backend/app/services/earnings_live_service.py:554`
- **What:** `refresh()` (lines 602-615) prunes `_records`/`_fetched` down to {previous trading day, today} only after `_run_once`. It returns before pruning when `due_days` is empty (line 607) or while the failure wait is on (line 604). `landed_snapshots` (546-559) returns every record that has `landed_at` and `landed_from_pending`, whatever its day. Its docstring says "previous trading day and today", which the code does not enforce. At the start of every round, `earnings_live_sender._prune` (sender line 140) drops `_live_done` / `_surprise_done` keys older than the previous trading day. So a landed record that outlives the day roll, because no fetch was due, is handed back each tick. The sender sees it as new, re-adds it and calls `notify_watchers` again. This repeats every 60 s until some later refresh happens to prune. `test_records_outside_the_two_live_days_are_pruned_at_the_day_roll` passes only because its fetch is due. `test_the_once_per_report_memory_is_bounded_to_the_live_days` assumes the service never hands back old records.
- **Failure scenario:** Reproduced (scratchpad review/test_repro_stale_landings.py). Thu 2026-10-29: INTC is seen pending, then FMP shows EPS 0.451 vs 0.41 (+10%). Friday's watched reports are all complete by midnight. On Saturday `due_days` is [] (prev=Fri is complete and Saturday has no rows), so nothing is pruned. Three ticks on Saturday made three `earnings_result` dispatches for INTC 2026-10-29. In production each one is a paged `watchers_of` read, recipient resolution and a `claim_send` per eligible watcher, repeated every minute from Saturday 00:00 to Monday's first due fetch. Because the own fetch keeps every company on the calendar (see the market-wide finding), the sender's 60-per-round cap is filled every minute (about 190k notify calls per weekend). Users who were over the daily cap on Thursday, or who added INTC since, get "INTC earnings are out, EPS beat consensus by 10%" on Saturday for Thursday's report. The same window opens every weekday from 00:00 to 06:00 ET when all of the previous day's watched reports are complete.
- **Suggested fix:** Limit `landed_snapshots` to `rec.day in {previous_trading_day(today), today}`. Also prune `_records`/`_fetched` at the top of every `refresh()`/tick, whether or not a fetch is due or the failure wait is on. Add a test with an empty `due_days` across the day roll that checks the sender is not called again.

### R2. [CONFIRMED] [medium] Duplicate (symbol, date) rows are folded one by one: a pending+reported pair counts as a landing on first sight, and one row's estimate gets paired with another row's actual

- **Where:** `backend/app/services/earnings_live_service.py:498`
- **What:** `observe_rows` folds rows one at a time and `_note_landing` runs after each row. When FMP returns its duplicate shape (a stale pending row and the reported row for the same symbol and date), the pending row sets `seen_pending` and the reported row, folded next, sets `landed_from_pending=True`. So a record that is already reported the first time this process sees it (after a restart, or the first fetch of the previous day) counts as a landing. That breaks the rule the docstring claims to share with `earnings_window_service._absorb`, which treats such a batch as plain "reported". `test_a_reschedule_duplicate_pair_reads_as_reported` checks only the status, not the landing. `_fold` also overwrites field by field and the last row wins, so `eps_estimate`/`revenue_estimate` can come from one row while `fmp_eps`/`fmp_revenue` come from the other.
- **Failure scenario:** Reproduced (review/test_repro_duplicate_pair_landing.py). A fresh process at 17:00 sees [pending INTC, reported INTC 0.44/17.1B], and `landed_snapshots` returns INTC. After a deploy, the sender pushes again for a report this process never saw land: `earnings_result` to users who were not claimed, and, once EARNINGS_LIVE_PUSH_ENABLED is on, "INTC results are out" hours later. With rows [reported (est 0.41, act 0.44, rev est 16.6B, act 17.1B), stale pending (est 0.35, rev est 15.9B)], the snapshot shows EPS surprise +25.71% (the true figure is +7.3%) and revenue +7.55% (true +3.0%). The card shows that next to "EPS Est: $0.41" taken from the row, and the live result push would say "EPS beat consensus by 26%".
- **Suggested fix:** Fold each (symbol, day) group as one unit. Decide whether any row in the batch is reported before setting `seen_pending`, the way `_absorb` does. Take estimate and actual from the same row (prefer the reported row or the newest `lastUpdated`) instead of overwriting field by field.

The all-day figure (~1,300 calls for a release before the open) needs FMP to lag until 22:00 ET or never fill revenue. - **No throttle risk.** Three requests a minute against a 5 rps limiter cannot trip the shared SEC limit or the 600 s circuit, so that part of the claim is overstated. - **The streamed bytes are decoded bytes.** The 3 MB cap counts decoded bytes, so a gzip body moves far less over the wire. - **The log noise does not reach Sentry.** The WARNING lines are breadcrumbs only, because Sentry's `event_level` is ERROR. - **No user-visible effect.** The FMP path is unaffected, and the filing figures could never be read anyway. - **The trigger is rare.** Typical EX-99.1 documents are 20–300 KB, against a 3 MB cap. It is still a genuine defect worth a small fix: wasted SEC calls (about 3x a not-yet-found symbol's per-round cost, for hours), a WARNING every round, and a broken documented contract. The fix is to catch `SECEdgarDocumentTooLargeException` around the exhibit read in `_extract`, log `result=exhibit_too_large` and return `(None, True)`, so the accession becomes final.
