## Section 1 — Two Blank Cans

**1.** "The object above is a pair of bare aluminium cans, beaded with condensation, with nothing printed on either. The only difference is the light: one is lit cold blue, the other warm amber."
- Description of the article's cover picture (per the assignment). No factual claim about either company.

**2.** "In the can, the two famous colas are near-twins, each made from a flavour concentrate, sweetener and carbonated water."
- [KO] Item 1, p. 3: "“concentrates” means flavorings and other ingredients which, when combined with water and, depending on the product, sweeteners (nutritive or non-nutritive) are used to prepare syrups or finished beverages"; and "Our bottling partners combine concentrates with still or sparkling water and sweeteners (depending on the product), or combine syrups with still or sparkling water, to produce finished beverages."
- [KO-1886]: "Carbonated water was teamed with the new syrup to produce a drink"
- [PEP] Item 1, p. 3: "IB Franchise makes, markets and sells beverage concentrates to authorized and independent bottlers under various beverage brands including 7UP, Aquafina, Gatorade, Mirinda, Mountain Dew, Pepsi, Pepsi Black, Pepsi Zero Sugar, and Sting Energy."
- [PEP] Item 1, p. 4: "The principal ingredients we use in our beverage and convenient food products are acesulfame potassium, aspartame, … corn sweeteners, flavorings, … sucralose, sugar, … We also use water in the manufacturing of our products." Glossary (Item 7): "CSD: carbonated soft drinks."
- "Near-twins" is the article's framing, not a sourced taste claim; no taste-test or preference claim is made.

**3.** "One mostly sells the flavour and the name, and leaves the heavy lifting to partners."
- [KO] Item 7, p. 37: concentrate operations = 59% of net operating revenues and "Concentrate operations | 85 | %" of worldwide unit case volume (2025).
- [KO] Item 1, p. 3: concentrates = "flavorings and other ingredients"; p. 7: "Most of our branded beverage products are prepared, packaged, distributed and sold by independent bottling partners."

**4.** "The other makes, bottles and delivers a large share of what it sells"
- [PEP] Item 1, p. 2: "we make, market, distribute and sell a wide variety of beverages and convenient foods"; p. 3: "PBNA operates its own bottling plants and distribution facilities"; Note 1, p. 67 fn (a): beverage revenue from company-owned bottlers "is 36% of our consolidated net revenue in 2025".

**5.** "and takes most of its revenue from chips, cereals and other foods."
- [PEP] Note 1, p. 67: convenient foods 58% of 2025 net revenue (see H3).
- [PEP] Item 1, p. 3: "PFNA makes, markets, distributes and sells convenient foods, which include cereals, chips, dips, granola bars, oatmeal, pasta, rice and syrups and mixes"

## Section 2 — The Syrup Company

**6.** "Coca-Cola began in Atlanta in 1886 as a syrup, mixed with carbonated water at a pharmacy soda fountain."
- [KO-1886]: "The product that has given the world its best-known taste was born in Atlanta, Georgia, on May 8, 1886. … a local pharmacist, produced the syrup for Coca-Cola, and carried a jug of the new product down the street to Jacobs' Pharmacy, where it was sampled, pronounced "excellent" and placed on sale for five cents a glass as a soda fountain drink. Carbonated water was teamed with the new syrup to produce a drink"
- [KO] Item 1, p. 2: "beverage products bearing our trademarks, sold in the United States since 1886"

**7.** "In a sense it never stopped being a syrup company." — framing line; supported by claims 8 and 11 (concentrate and syrup operations are 59% of revenue).

**8.** "Its annual report describes two lines of business: concentrate operations, selling concentrate and syrup to bottlers, and finished product operations, selling finished drinks."
- [KO] Item 1, p. 3: "Our Company operates in two lines of business: concentrate operations and finished product operations. Our concentrate operations typically generate net operating revenues by selling beverage concentrates, sometimes referred to as “beverage bases,” syrups, including fountain syrups, and certain finished beverages to authorized bottling operations"
- [KO] Item 1, p. 4: "Our finished product operations generate net operating revenues by selling sparkling soft drinks and a variety of other finished beverages to retailers, or to distributors and wholesalers who in turn sell the beverages to retailers."

**9.** "Most of its drinks are made and sold by independent bottlers."
- [KO] Item 1 (Investments in Bottling Operations), p. 7: "Most of our branded beverage products are prepared, packaged, distributed and sold by independent bottling partners."

**10.** "Under bottler's agreements, each buys its concentrate from Coca-Cola, adds water and sweetener, packages the drink and sells it in an assigned territory."
- [KO] Item 1 (Bottler's Agreements), p. 5: "the bottler's agreements generally authorize the bottlers to prepare, package, distribute and sell Company Trademark Beverages in authorized containers in (but, subject to applicable local law, generally only in) an identified territory. The bottler is obligated to purchase its entire requirement of concentrates or syrups for the designated Company Trademark Beverages from the Company or Company-authorized suppliers."
- [KO] Item 1, p. 3: "Our bottling partners combine concentrates with still or sparkling water and sweeteners (depending on the product) … The finished beverages are packaged in authorized containers, such as cans and refillable and nonrefillable glass and plastic bottles"

**11.** "In fiscal 2025 the five largest accounted for 44% of worldwide volume."
- [KO] Item 1, p. 5: "Our five largest independent bottling partners based on unit case volume in 2025 were as follows: … In 2025, these five bottling partners combined represented 44% of our total worldwide unit case volume."

**12.** "Concentrate made up 59% of Coca-Cola's revenue of about $47.9 billion in fiscal 2025"
- [KO] Item 7, p. 37: "Concentrate operations | 59 | %" (2025 share of total net operating revenues).
- [KO] Item 8, Consolidated Statements of Income, p. 61: "Net Operating Revenues | $ | 47,941 | $ | 47,061 | $ | 45,754" (in millions; 2025 | 2024 | 2023). Also Note 3, p. 75: "Total | $ | 19,127 | $ | 28,814 | $ | 47,941" (2025, U.S. | International | Total). $47,941 million ≈ $47.9 billion.

**13.** "and 85% of its volume."
- [KO] Item 7, p. 37: "The following table sets forth the percentage of total worldwide unit case volume attributable to concentrate operations and finished product operations: Year Ended December 31, | 2025 | 2024 | Concentrate operations | 85 | % | 85 | %"

**14.** "The filing states the trade-off plainly: finished drinks bring in more revenue, but at lower gross margins."
- [KO] Item 1, p. 4: "Generally, finished product operations generate higher net operating revenues but lower gross profit margins than concentrate operations."

**15.** "It calls its core business building brands and leading its system of bottlers."
- [KO] Item 1A, p. 19: "As part of our strategic initiative to focus on our core business of building brands and leading our system of bottling partners, we continue to seek opportunities to refranchise our consolidated bottling operations."

**16.** "So it keeps selling company-owned bottling back to independent partners, which it calls refranchising."
- [KO] Item 1A, p. 19 (above): "we continue to seek opportunities to refranchise our consolidated bottling operations."
- [KO] Item 1, p. 7: "In line with our long-term bottling strategy, we may periodically consider options for divesting or reducing our ownership interest in a consolidated bottling operation, typically by selling all or a portion of our interest in the bottling operation to an independent bottler to improve Coca-Cola system efficiency."

**17.** "In 2025 it refranchised bottling in parts of India"
- [KO] Note 2 (Acquisitions and Divestitures), p. 73: "In May 2025, the Company refranchised our bottling operations in certain territories in India that were held for sale as of December 31, 2024, for which we received cash proceeds of $218 million"

**18.** "sold its finished product operations in Nigeria"
- [KO] Note 2, p. 73: "In October 2025, we sold our finished product operations in Nigeria, which were classified as held for sale, for which we received cash proceeds of $106 million."

**19.** "and agreed to sell part of its African bottling to one of its largest bottlers."
- [KO] Note 2, p. 74: "In October 2025, the Company entered into a definitive agreement to sell a portion of our interest in our bottling operations in Africa to CCHBC, an equity method investee."
- [KO] Item 1, p. 5: CCHBC is listed among "Our five largest independent bottling partners based on unit case volume in 2025": "Coca-Cola HBC AG (“CCHBC”), which has bottling and distribution operations in Armenia, Austria, …"

**20.** "Coca-Cola's own history shows how much rests on the name."
- Framing line. [KO-1985]: "What these tests didn't show, of course, was the bond consumers felt with their Coca‑Cola — something they didn't want anyone, including The Coca‑Cola Company, tampering with."

**21.** "In April 1985 it announced the first formula change in 99 years"
- [KO-1985]: "To hear some tell it, April 23, 1985, was a day that will live in marketing infamy. On that day, The Coca‑Cola Company … announcing that it was changing the formula for the world's most popular soft drink"; and "The Coca‑Cola Company introduced reformulated Coca‑Cola, often referred to as "new Coke," marking the first formula change in 99 years."

**22.** "a taste preferred in tests of nearly 200,000 consumers."
- [KO-1985]: "The fabled secret formula for Coca‑Cola was changed, adopting a formula preferred in taste tests of nearly 200,000 consumers."

**23.** "After a firestorm of consumer protest, the original formula returned 79 days later as Coca-Cola classic."
- [KO-1985]: "The company didn't set out to create the firestorm of consumer protest that ensued"; "That firestorm ended with the return of the original formula, now called Coca‑Cola classic, a few months later. The return of original formula Coca‑Cola on July 11, 1985, put the cap on 79 days that revolutionized the soft-drink industry"
- Check: April 23 → July 11, 1985 = 7 + 31 + 30 + 11 = 79 days.

## Section 3 — The Company That Makes Things

**24.** "PepsiCo was formed in 1965, when Frito-Lay merged with Pepsi-Cola"
- [PEP-1965]: "In 1965, Frito-Lay, Inc. merged with Pepsi-Cola to form PepsiCo, giving the brand even more national reach"

**25.** "so snacks were there from its first day."
- [PEP-1965]: "In 1961, it merged with the Frito Company — which, like Lay's, was founded in 1932 and sold snacks that are still enjoyed today, including its namesake Fritos. The new company became known as Frito-Lay, Inc." (followed by the 1965 merger sentence in claim 24).

**26.** "In fiscal 2025 foods were the larger half: convenient foods made up 58% of net revenue … beverages 42%."
- [PEP] Note 1, p. 67: "PepsiCo | 42 | % | 58 | %" (2025: Beverages(a) | Convenient Foods).

**27.** "net revenue of about $93.9 billion"
- [PEP] Item 8, Consolidated Statement of Income, p. 57: "Net Revenue | $ | 93,925 | $ | 91,854 | $ | 91,471" (in millions; fiscal 2025 | 2024 | 2023). $93,925 million ≈ $93.9 billion.

**28.** "Its six reporting segments follow that mix:"
- [PEP] Item 1, p. 2: "We are organized into six reportable segments, as follows:"

**29.** Bullet: "Two North American units, one for convenient foods and one for beverages"
- [PEP] Item 1, p. 2: "1)PepsiCo Foods North America (PFNA), which includes all of our convenient food businesses in the United States and Canada; 2)PepsiCo Beverages North America (PBNA), which includes all of our beverage businesses in the United States and Canada;"

**30.** Bullet: "A franchise unit that sells drink concentrate to bottlers internationally"
- [PEP] Item 1, p. 2: "3)International Beverages Franchise (IB Franchise), which includes our international franchise beverage businesses, as well as our SodaStream business;"
- [PEP] Item 1, p. 3: "IB Franchise makes, markets and sells beverage concentrates to authorized and independent bottlers"

**31.** Bullet: "A Europe, Middle East and Africa unit running both foods and company-owned bottlers"
- [PEP] Item 1, p. 2: "4)Europe, Middle East and Africa (EMEA), which includes our convenient food businesses and our beverage businesses with company-owned bottlers in Europe, the Middle East and Africa;"

**32.** Bullet: "Food-only units for Latin America and Asia Pacific"
- [PEP] Item 1, p. 3: "5)Latin America Foods (LatAm Foods), which includes all of our convenient food businesses in Latin America; and 6)Asia Pacific Foods, which consists of our convenient food businesses in Asia Pacific, including China, Australia and New Zealand, as well as India."

**33.** "Where Coca-Cola mostly leaves bottling to partners, PepsiCo does much of it itself."
- [KO] Item 1, p. 7: "Most of our branded beverage products are prepared, packaged, distributed and sold by independent bottling partners."
- [PEP] Note 1, p. 67 fn (a): beverage revenue from company-owned bottlers "is 36% of our consolidated net revenue in 2025"; Item 1, p. 3: "PBNA operates its own bottling plants and distribution facilities" and "EMEA operates its own bottling plants and distribution facilities and sells finished goods directly to independent distributors and retailers."

**34.** "Its North American drinks business runs its own bottling plants"
- [PEP] Item 1, p. 3: "PBNA operates its own bottling plants and distribution facilities and sells branded finished goods directly to independent distributors and retailers."

**35.** "and direct store delivery, run with its bottlers and distributors, carries drinks and foods straight to stores, where its staff or bottlers stock the shelves."
- [PEP] Item 1 (Direct-Store-Delivery), p. 4: "We, our independent bottlers and our distributors operate DSD systems that deliver beverages and convenient foods directly to retail stores where the products are merchandised by our employees or our independent bottlers."

**36.** "In fiscal 2025, company-owned bottlers produced 36% of its revenue."
- [PEP] Note 1, p. 67, footnote (a): "Beverage revenue from company-owned bottlers, which primarily includes our consolidated bottling operations in our PBNA and EMEA segments, is 36% of our consolidated net revenue in 2025 and 35% of our consolidated net revenue in both 2024 and 2023."

## Section 4 — What Each Structure Buys

**37.** "In fiscal 2025 PepsiCo took in roughly twice Coca-Cola's revenue"
- Computed from claims 12 and 27: $93,925 million ÷ $47,941 million = 1.96.

**38.** "but it employed about 306,000 people"
- [PEP] Item 1 (Human Capital), p. 10: "We employed approximately 306,000 people worldwide as of December 27, 2025, including approximately 125,000 people within the United States."

**39.** "against Coca-Cola's 65,900"
- [KO] Item 1 (Human Capital), p. 10: "As of December 31, 2025 and 2024, our Company had approximately 65,900 and 69,700 employees, respectively"

**40.** "more than four times as many."
- Computed: 306,000 ÷ 65,900 = 4.64.

**41.** "Running its own bottling and delivery gives PepsiCo more control over how products reach the shelf, and one route into stores for drinks and foods."
- [PEP] Item 1, p. 4: "We, our independent bottlers and our distributors operate DSD systems that deliver beverages and convenient foods directly to retail stores where the products are merchandised by our employees or our independent bottlers. DSD enables us to merchandise with maximum visibility and appeal. DSD is especially well-suited to products that are restocked often and respond to in-store promotion and merchandising."
- [PEP] Item 1, p. 3: "PBNA operates its own bottling plants and distribution facilities". (The "control" wording is the article's reading of these statements.)

**42.** "Selling concentrate gives Coca-Cola a leaner business built on its brands"
- Article's reading of: claim 39 (65,900 employees vs PepsiCo's 306,000), claim 12 (59% of revenue from concentrate) and [KO] Item 1A, p. 19: "our core business of building brands and leading our system of bottling partners".

**43.** "but leaves it reliant on bottlers that are independent contractors, not its agents."
- [KO] Item 1, p. 5: "Being a bottler does not create a legal partnership or joint venture between us and our bottlers. Our bottlers are independent contractors and are not our agents."
- [KO] Item 1A, p. 19: "if our refranchising partners are not efficient or not aligned with our long-term vision for the Coca-Cola system, our business and results of operations could be adversely affected."

**44.** Callout: "Two companies can sell almost the same product and still be very different businesses. The model lives in two lines of the annual report: where the revenue comes from, and who owns the plants and the trucks."
- Transferable lesson; no factual claim. (The "two lines" correspond to KO's concentrate/finished-product revenue table and PEP's beverage/convenient-food and company-owned-bottler disclosures, claims 12–13, 26, 36.)

**45.** "Coca-Cola's lean figures leave out its bottlers, whose plants and people sit on their own books"
- [KO] Item 1, p. 2: "In this report, the terms “The Coca-Cola Company,” “Company,” “we,” “us” and “our” mean The Coca-Cola Company and all entities included in our consolidated financial statements."
- [KO] Item 1, p. 5: "Our bottlers are independent contractors and are not our agents." (Independent bottlers are not consolidated, so their employees and plants are outside the 65,900 figure.)

**46.** "companies in which it holds sizeable stakes, including several large bottlers, had combined revenue of about $102.8 billion in 2025."
- [KO] Note 6 (Equity Method Investments), p. 85: "The Company’s equity method investments include, but are not limited to, our ownership interests in CCEP; Monster; AC Bebidas, S. de R.L. de C.V.; Coca-Cola FEMSA; CCHBC; and Coca-Cola Bottlers Japan Holdings Inc. As of December 31, 2025, we owned 18%, 21%, 20%, 28%, 22% and 24%, respectively, of these companies’ outstanding shares."; "A summary of financial information for our equity method investees in the aggregate is as follows (in millions): … Net operating revenues | $ | 102,800" (2025). Footnote: "The financial information represents the results of the equity method investees during the Company’s period of ownership."
- CCEP, Coca-Cola FEMSA and CCHBC are three of the five largest independent bottlers ([KO] Item 1, p. 5). The aggregate also includes Monster (not a bottler), hence "including several large bottlers", not "all bottlers".

**47.** "Coca-Cola still owns some bottling"
- [KO] Item 1, p. 4: "These operations consist primarily of our consolidated bottling and distribution operations, which are included in our Bottling Investments operating segment."
- [KO] Item 2 (Properties), p. 28, principal facilities table: "Bottling Investments | — | — | 56 | 5 | 53 | 68 | — | —" (columns: concentrate/syrup plants owned | leased | beverage manufacturing/bottling plants owned | leased | distribution and storage owned | leased | retail owned | leased).

**48.** "and PepsiCo also sells concentrate to franchise bottlers."
- [PEP] Item 1, p. 3: "IB Franchise makes, markets and sells beverage concentrates to authorized and independent bottlers"; "PBNA also sells concentrate and finished goods for our brands to authorized and independent bottlers, who in turn sell our branded finished goods to independent distributors and retailers in certain markets."

**49.** "Neither model is simply better; each pays for a different kind of control." — editorial conclusion; no factual claim.

## Considered and dropped

- **Coca-Cola's 1899 bottling-rights deal (rights sold for $1).** Could not find it on Coca-Cola's own English-language history pages (the timeline loads dynamically and the candidate URL returns 404); found only on secondary sites and a non-English Coca-Cola site. Dropped.
- **Property, plant and equipment comparison** (PepsiCo $29,905M vs Coca-Cola $9,613M at fiscal 2025 year-end, both sourced). Dropped because Coca-Cola's year-end figure excludes the African bottling assets reclassified as held for sale (Note 2 table: PP&E held for sale $1,964M), which would overstate the gap.
- **U.S. market-share figures** (PepsiCo 10-K Item 1 p. 7 cites IRI: PepsiCo ~16%, Coca-Cola ~20% of U.S. liquid refreshment beverages) and Coca-Cola's 1985 page's remark that its share lead "had been slowly slipping for 15 consecutive years": both primary-sourced, left out to keep the piece on business models rather than who is winning.
- **Gross margin comparison** (Coca-Cola states 61.6% for 2025; PepsiCo's would have to be computed): left out in favour of each filing's own statement of the concentrate-versus-finished-goods margin trade-off.
