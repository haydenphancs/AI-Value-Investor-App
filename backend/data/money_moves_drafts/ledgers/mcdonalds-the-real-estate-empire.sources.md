## Header fields

**H1. subtitle:** "A burger company whose franchise contract is also a lease, so that a large part of what it earns arrives as rent from the operators who run restaurants on property it controls."
- [10-K] Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements, "Franchise Arrangements", p. 53: "Conventional franchise arrangements generally include a lease and a license and provide for payment of initial fees, as well as continuing rent and royalties to the Company based upon a percent of sales with minimum rent payments."
- "A large part" is derived. Rents were $10,442 million of $26,885 million total revenues, about 39% ([10-K] p. 53 "Rents | $ | 10,442"; Consolidated Statement of Income p. 39 "Total revenues | 26,885").
- "Property it controls": [10-K] MD&A, "Sources of Liquidity", p. 23: "The Company believes that control over the real estate enables it to achieve restaurant performance levels that are among the highest in the industry."

**H2. cardSubtitle:** "Why a burger brand is also a landlord."
- [10-K] "Franchise Arrangements", p. 53: "McDonald's has elected to allocate consideration in the franchise contract among lease and non-lease components in the same manner that it has historically: rental income (lease), royalty income (non-lease) and initial fee income (non-lease)."

## Body

### Section 1: "A Building on a Plot"

**1.** "The object above is a blueprint, white lines on blue, of a small roadside restaurant drawn as an exploded assembly. … There is no sign anywhere, and that absence is the point."
- Describes the commissioned cover illustration, as specified in the assignment. It makes no factual claim about the company. The link it draws to the company ("no sign": the franchisee supplies the signs) rests on claim 2.

**2.** "Under a conventional franchise, the company generally owns or holds a long-term lease on the land and the building. The franchisee pays for the equipment, the signs, the seating and the décor."
- [10-K] Item 1, "Conventional Franchise", p. 3: "Under a conventional franchise arrangement, the Company generally owns or secures a long-term lease on the land and building for the restaurant location and the franchisee pays for equipment, signs, seating and décor."

**3.** "In McDonald's own filings, a large part of the business sits underneath both."
- Interpretive framing. It is supported by claims 22 and 28 (rent is the largest franchised revenue line; a large owned property base) and by [10-K] Item 1A, p. 30: "We have significant real estate operations, primarily in connection with our restaurant business."

### Section 2: "Two Kinds of Franchise"

**4.** "McDonald's traces its own beginning to a restaurant in Des Plaines, Illinois, in 1955."
- [PROXY] Chairman's letter, "Building Upon a Legacy of Innovation and Leadership": "Our founder, Ray Kroc, opened the first McDonald's in Des Plaines, Illinois, on April 15, 1955, revolutionizing the way people experienced quick-service dining." The same letter continues: "But our 70-year legacy is not just about longevity; it's about leadership." The article does not name the person and states the date only as the company's own account.

**5.** "By the end of 2025 there were 45,356 McDonald's restaurants, and about 95% were franchised."
- [10-K] Item 1, p. 3: "Of the 45,356 McDonald's restaurants at year-end 2025, approximately 95% were franchised."
- Also [10-K] Note "Nature of Business", p. 44: "Total Systemwide restaurants | 45,356 | 43,477 | 41,822".

**6.** "But in the annual report franchised covers two quite different deals, and only one of them makes the company a landlord."
- [10-K] Item 1, p. 3: "McDonald's franchised restaurants are owned and operated under one of the following structures - conventional franchise, developmental license or affiliate."
- [10-K] Item 1, p. 4: "While developmental license and affiliate arrangements are largely the same, …" This is why the article treats license and affiliate as one kind of deal.
- "Only one … landlord": conventional franchises include a lease (claim H1, p. 53), while licensees and affiliates pay a royalty and supply their own real estate (claim 8).

**7.** "Roughly 20,700 restaurants were run by developmental licensees and affiliates, …"
- [10-K] Note "Nature of Business", p. 44, table "Restaurants at December 31": "Developmental licensed | 9,675 | 9,247 | 8,684" and "Foreign affiliated | 11,072 | 10,108 | 9,178" (2025 | 2024 | 2023).
- Derived: 9,675 + 11,072 = 20,747, rounded to "roughly 20,700".

**8.** "… who supply their own capital, including the real estate, and pay a royalty on sales."
- [10-K] Item 1, "Developmental License or Affiliate", p. 4: "Under a developmental license or affiliate arrangement, licensees are responsible for operating and managing their businesses, providing capital (including the real estate interest) and developing and opening new restaurants. The Company generally does not invest any restaurant capital under a developmental license or affiliate arrangement, and it receives a royalty based on a percent of sales, …"

**9.** "About 22,600 ran under conventional franchises, …"
- [10-K] p. 44: "Conventional franchised | 22,570 | 22,077 | 21,818" (2025 first).

**10.** "… and the company operated about 2,000 itself."
- [10-K] p. 44: "Company-owned and operated | 2,039 | 2,045 | 2,142" (2025 first).

**11.** "On those sites it generally controls the property."
- [10-K] Item 2, "Properties", p. 36: "The Company generally owns or secures a long-term lease on the land and building for conventional franchised and Company-owned and operated restaurant sites, which facilitates long-term occupancy rights and helps control related costs."

**12.** Subheading "How a conventional franchisee pays". Bullet: "A royalty, set as a percent of sales"
- [10-K] Item 1, p. 4: "Conventional franchisees contribute to the Company's revenue, primarily through the payment of rent and royalties based upon a percent of sales, with specified minimum rent payments, along with initial fees paid upon the opening of a new restaurant or grant of a new franchise."
- [10-K] Revenue Recognition, p. 45: "Royalty revenues are based on a percent of sales and recognized at the time the underlying sales occur."

**13.** Bullet: "Rent, also tied to a percent of sales, with a specified minimum"
- [10-K] Item 1, p. 4 (same sentence as claim 12): "… rent and royalties based upon a percent of sales, with specified minimum rent payments …"
- [10-K] Revenue Recognition, p. 45: "Rental income includes both minimum rent payments, which are recognized straight-line over the franchise term and variable rent payments based on a percent of sales, which are recognized at the time the underlying sales occur."

**14.** Bullet: "An initial fee when a restaurant opens or a new franchise is granted"
- [10-K] Item 1, p. 4: "… along with initial fees paid upon the opening of a new restaurant or grant of a new franchise."

**15.** Bullet: "And, generally, the site's property taxes, insurance and maintenance"
- [10-K] "Franchise Arrangements", p. 53: "Franchisees generally pay related occupancy costs including property taxes, insurance and site maintenance."

**16.** "The minimum rent is anchored to the property. The filing says it is based on the company's investment in sites it owns, and parallels its own lease payments and escalations on sites it leases."
- [10-K] "Franchise Arrangements", p. 53: "Minimum rent payments are based on the Company's underlying investment in owned sites and parallel the Company's underlying leases and escalations on properties that are leased."
- Also [10-K] MD&A, "Sources of Liquidity", p. 23: "Minimum rent payments under franchise arrangements are based on the Company's underlying investment in owned sites and parallel the Company's underlying lease obligations and escalations on properties that are leased."

**17.** "A franchise generally runs 20 years."
- [10-K] "Franchise Arrangements", p. 53: "… franchisees are granted the right to operate a restaurant using the McDonald's System and, in most cases, the use of a restaurant facility, generally for a period of 20 years."
- Also [10-K] Item 1, p. 3: "… stipulated in franchise/license agreements that generally have 20-year terms."

**18.** "At the end, the company keeps control of the land and building, and can renew with the same operator, choose a different one, or close the restaurant."
- [10-K] p. 53: "At the end of the 20-year franchise arrangement, the Company maintains control of the underlying real estate and building and can either enter into a new 20-year franchise arrangement with the existing franchisee or a different franchisee, or close the restaurant."

### Section 3: "Rent Is the Bigger Line"

**19.** "In fiscal 2025 McDonald's took in about $26.9 billion."
- [10-K] Consolidated Statement of Income, p. 39: "Total revenues | 26,885 | 25,920 | 25,494" (2025 | 2024 | 2023, millions).
- Also MD&A revenues table, p. 14: "Total Revenues | $ | 26,885 | …".
- [10-K] Item 1, p. 3 confirms the fiscal year: "For the year ended December 31, 2025, …"

**20.** "About $16.5 billion came from franchised restaurants, …"
- [10-K] p. 39: "Revenues from franchised restaurants | $ | 16,548 | $ | 15,715 | $ | 15,437".

**21.** "… against about $9.7 billion of sales at restaurants it ran itself."
- [10-K] p. 39: "Sales by Company-owned and operated restaurants | 9,690 | 9,782 | 9,742".
- The other $647 million is "Other revenues | 647 | 423 | 316" (p. 39). The article does not claim the two lines add up to the total.

**22.** "Inside the franchised figure, rent was about $10.4 billion and royalties about $6.0 billion."
- [10-K] "Franchise Arrangements", p. 53: "Revenues from franchised restaurants consisted of: … Rents | $ | 10,442 | … Royalties | 6,018 | … Initial fees | 88 | … Revenues from franchised restaurants | $ | 16,548" (2025 column).

**23.** "The landlord line beat the brand line by more than $4 billion."
- Derived from claim 22: $10,442 million minus $6,018 million = $4,424 million.

**24.** "The franchise side is also where the restaurant profit sits. After occupancy costs, which include lease expense and depreciation, …"
- [10-K] MD&A, "Restaurant Margins", p. 15: "Franchised restaurant margins are measured as revenues from franchised restaurants less franchised restaurant occupancy costs. Franchised restaurant occupancy costs include lease expense and depreciation, as the Company generally owns or secures a long-term lease on the land and building for the restaurant location."

**25.** "… franchised margins came to about $13.9 billion, …"
- [10-K] MD&A, "Restaurant margins" table, p. 15: "Franchised: … Total | $ | 13,930 | $ | 13,178 | $ | 12,962" (2025 first, millions).
- Cross-check: $16,548 million franchised revenues less $2,618 million "Franchised restaurants-occupancy expenses" (p. 39) = $13,930 million.

**26.** "… roughly 90% of all restaurant margin dollars."
- [10-K] p. 15: "Franchised margins represented approximately 90% of restaurant margin dollars."

**27.** "Behind them is a large property book."
- [10-K] "Franchise Arrangements", p. 53: "At December 31, 2025, net property and equipment under franchise arrangements totaled $22.8 billion (including land of $7.1 billion) after deducting accumulated depreciation and amortization of $16.9 billion."

**28.** "The company owned about 56% of the land and about 80% of the buildings for restaurants in its consolidated markets, …"
- [10-K] MD&A, "Restaurant Development and Capital Expenditures", p. 21: "As of December 31, 2025 and 2024, the Company owned approximately 56% of the land and approximately 80% of the buildings for restaurants in its consolidated markets."

**29.** "… and existing conventional franchises carried about $31.5 billion of future minimum rent."
- [10-K] "Franchise Arrangements", p. 53: "Future gross minimum rent payments due to the Company under existing conventional franchise arrangements are: … Total minimum payments | $ | 15,843 | $ | 15,608 | $ | 31,451" (Owned sites | Leased sites | Total, millions).

**30.** Callout (highlight): "To understand a franchise business, ask who holds the ground. A brand alone collects a share of sales. A brand that is also the landlord collects rent with a floor under it, and keeps the site when the contract ends."
- This is the transferable lesson, an editorial generalisation. Its company-specific parts rest on claims 8 and 12 (licensees: royalty on sales only), claim 13 (rent with a specified minimum, the "floor") and claim 18 (control of the site at term end).

### Section 4: "What the Landlord Carries"

**31.** "Holding the ground is not free. Much of it is leased rather than owned: …"
- Derived from claim 28: the company owned about 56% of the land and about 80% of the buildings in consolidated markets, so the rest is held another way.
- [10-K] "Leasing Arrangements", p. 54: "The Company is the lessee in a significant real estate portfolio, …"

**32.** "… the company is a tenant across a large portfolio, leasing the land alone at some sites and the land and building at others."
- [10-K] "Leasing Arrangements", p. 54 (same wording in "Leasing", Summary of Significant Accounting Policies, p. 46): "The Company is the lessee in a significant real estate portfolio, primarily through ground leases (the Company leases the land and generally owns the building) and through improved leases (the Company leases the land and buildings)."

**33.** "At the end of 2025 its lease liabilities stood at about $14.8 billion, …"
- [10-K] "Leasing Arrangements", lease maturity table, p. 55: "Present value of lease liability | $ | 12,488 | $ | 2,352 | $ | 14,840" (Operating | Finance | Total, millions).
- Cross-check: balance-sheet detail, p. 54, December 31, 2025: "Current lease liability | 671 | 23 | 694" and "Long-term lease liability | 11,817 | 2,329 | 14,147" (694 + 14,147 = 14,841).

**34.** "… the present value of the minimum lease payments it expects to make."
- [10-K] "Leasing Arrangements", p. 54: "The Lease right-of-use asset and Lease liability reflect the present value of the Company's estimated future minimum lease payments over the lease term, which includes options that are reasonably certain of being exercised, discounted using a collateralized incremental borrowing rate."
- The article says "expects to make" rather than "owes" because the figure includes renewal options not yet exercised. The 10-K, p. 54: "… include an assumption on renewal options that have not yet been exercised by the Company, and are not currently a future obligation."

**35.** Callout (warning): "The percent-of-sales design cuts both ways. Royalties and the variable part of rent shrink when franchisee sales shrink, …"
- [10-K] Revenue Recognition, p. 45: "Royalty revenues are based on a percent of sales …" and "Rental income includes both minimum rent payments, … and variable rent payments based on a percent of sales …"
- That these lines fall when sales fall follows directly from their being a percent of sales. The minimum rent (claim 13) is the part that does not.

**36.** "… and the filing itself warns that its franchised revenue depends largely on franchisees growing their sales."
- [10-K] Item 1A, "Our franchise business model presents a number of risks.", p. 29: "The revenues we realize from franchised restaurants are largely dependent on the ability of our franchisees to grow their sales. Business risks affecting our operations also affect our franchisees. If franchisee sales trends worsen, or any of such risks materialize or intensify, our financial results could be negatively affected, which may be material."

**37.** "Two more honest limits. The model leans on franchisee economics: the company relies on operators willing and able to fund reinvestment, …"
- [10-K] Item 1A, p. 29: "Our success as a heavily franchised business relies to a large degree on the financial success and cooperation of our franchisees, …" and "Our success also relies on the willingness and ability of our franchisees and affiliates to implement major initiatives, which may include financial investment, and to remain aligned with us on operating, value/promotional and capital-intensive reinvestment plans."
- Also [10-K] Item 1, p. 3: "Franchisees are responsible for reinvesting capital in their businesses over time."

**38.** "… and may co-invest to modernise restaurants."
- [10-K] Item 1, p. 3: "In addition, to accelerate implementation of certain initiatives, the Company may co-invest with franchisees to fund improvements to their restaurants or operating systems. These investments, … are designed to … increase the value of the McDonald's brand through the development of modernized, more attractive and higher revenue generating restaurants."

**39.** "And property is slow to move. Long real estate commitments leave it limited flexibility to reshape the portfolio as trade areas and customer habits change, …"
- [10-K] Item 1A, "If we do not effectively manage our real estate portfolio, our operating results may be negatively impacted.", p. 30: "As we generally secure long-term real estate interests for our restaurants, we have limited flexibility to quickly alter our real estate portfolio. The competitive business landscape continues to evolve in light of changing business trends, consumer preferences, trade area demographics, consumer use of digital, delivery and drive thru, local competitive positions and other economic factors."

**40.** "… while the buildings it holds keep depreciating on its own books."
- [10-K] MD&A, p. 15: "Franchised restaurant occupancy costs include lease expense and depreciation, …"
- [10-K] p. 53: "… net property and equipment under franchise arrangements totaled $22.8 billion … after deducting accumulated depreciation and amortization of $16.9 billion."

## Considered and dropped

- The well-known line attributed to an early finance executive about the company being "in the real estate business". It is secondhand lore, excluded by the assignment, and neither used nor paraphrased.
- The founder, the two brothers and every executive go unnamed. The proxy's 1955 sentence names the founder, so the article states only the company's own dating of its first restaurant (claim 4).
- The assignment allowed "the first restaurant opened by the franchising company in 1955". The only page I could read was the proxy letter. McDonald's corporate web pages (the corporate history page, the US FAQ, a CEO-letter PDF) returned HTTP 403 to the fetcher. The proxy supports "opened the first McDonald's in Des Plaines, Illinois, on April 15, 1955" and a "70-year legacy". So the article says the company "traces its own beginning" to that restaurant. It does not say "the first McDonald's ever", because an earlier restaurant run by the brothers is commonly reported, and it does not mention the franchising company as an entity.
- Any claim that McDonald's "pioneered" the drive-through (the proxy says so, but the drive-through in the cover is only illustration, and the claim is not needed).
- Capital expenditure figures by type. In the 10-K text this table is a chart image, so no figure could be quoted verbatim.
